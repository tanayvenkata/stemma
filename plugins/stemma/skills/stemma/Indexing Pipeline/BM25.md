# BM25

BM25 is a lexical ranking family that rewards query-term matches using term rarity, saturating term frequency, and document-length normalization.

$$
\operatorname{score}(D,Q)=\sum_{q_i\in Q}\operatorname{IDF}(q_i)
\frac{f(q_i,D)(k_1+1)}{f(q_i,D)+k_1\left(1-b+b\frac{\lvert D\rvert}{\operatorname{avgdl}}\right)}
$$

- **IDF** gives more weight to terms rare in the indexed collection.
- **$k_1$** controls how quickly repeated occurrences stop adding much value.
- **$b$** controls the strength of length normalization.

Exact formulas, tokenization, analyzers, stopwords, stemming, field weighting, and default parameters vary by implementation. Treat them as part of the index contract.

## When It Helps

BM25 is a strong candidate for identifiers, names, error strings, statutes, jargon, quoted phrases, and corpora where exact vocabulary matters. Dense retrieval may be stronger for paraphrase and conceptual similarity. Either can win alone; hybrid fusion is an empirical option, not a mandatory architecture.

Compare lexical, dense, and fused rankings on pooled in-domain judgments. Tune analyzers and parameters only on development cases, then verify whether gains survive downstream context and generation.

Primary reference: [The Probabilistic Relevance Framework: BM25 and Beyond](https://www.staff.city.ac.uk/~sbrp622/papers/foundations_bm25_review.pdf) (Robertson & Zaragoza, 2009).
